## 1. Two more envelope sweeps — the rule holds at every geometry

The `overflow <= spare` rule was measured on 64 KB / 16-way (64 sets) on 25 Sep. It was re-measured at
two more geometries. **The cliff lands where the model says every time**, and the penalty for being
outside the region shrinks sharply as the set count rises.

Records: [64 KB 8-way](../performance/board-calib-envelope-8way-64kb-2026-09-25.md) ·
[1 MB 8-way](../performance/board-calib-envelope-8way-1mb-2026-09-26.md).

> ⚠️ **How not to read these.** `l2_miss_calib` fixes the **per-set** line count by construction: `-p 9`
> means 9 lines per hot set whether the cache is 64 KB or 1 MB. The sweep therefore scales the cache
> while holding the overflow constant, which is **not** what happens to a real program. These sweeps say
> *"if a workload has this shape, SBC helps at any size"* — they do **not** say *"a real workload will
> have this shape at that size"*. Section 3 is what happens when a real workload is asked.

## 2. One RTL fix was needed to build a 2048-set cache

`SBC_AtAssoc` in `Control.scala` computed its pad as `0.U((8 - sbcSetBits).W)`, which is a negative
width once a cache has more than 256 sets. The 1 MB config (2048 sets, 11 index bits) could not
elaborate. Fixed by widening the field to `(sbcSetBits max 8) + 1` and padding only when there is room.

**✅ Committed 2026-09-30 as `962fa05`** (tag `sbc-1mb-8way-linux-booted-2026-09-26`). Without it no config above 256 sets builds — baseline included, since the field is not gated by `enablePerfCounters`. It was dirty in **both** 1 MB builds, so the provenance files' commits (`c3faa05`, `de88ddc`) cannot rebuild those images; `962fa05` can.

| Counter | Value | Reading |

Two readings:

1. **The guests do not survive.** 617,145 migrations leave 2 lines parked. Arithmetic implies ~270,636
   migrations (44%) overwrote *another guest* — `migCommitReuse` has no MMIO counter, so that share is
   inferred, not measured.
2. **Dual core is the wrong test bed for this question.** Client-held aborts are 96.7% of all aborts —
   that is the second core's L1 holding the destination line, i.e. exactly the C3 case deliberately not
   built. A single-core re-run is owed before judging SBC at this geometry.
